# Parking lot

- If the control plane is unavailable, running apps keep running but nothing heals: "Components should continue to do what they were last told in the absence of new instructions (e.g., due to network partition or component outage)". Ch 14 (provider SLAs, control-plane outages) or a Ch 4 disclosure if a reader asks "what if the brain dies?" (https://github.com/kubernetes/design-proposals-archive/blob/main/architecture/principles.md)
- Eviction is rate-limited and stops entirely if most machines in a zone look unhealthy at once (the system assumes a network problem, not mass death): Ch 12 incidents / Ch 14 (https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/architecture/nodes/)
- Pods on an unreachable machine may keep running there, unseen, while their replacements start elsewhere ("the pods that are scheduled for deletion may continue to run on the partitioned node"): Ch 10 (why stateful apps need stable identities) (https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/taint-and-toleration/)
- Teams can shorten or lengthen the 5-minute wait per app (`tolerationSeconds`), e.g. longer for apps with lots of local state: Ch 5/10, engineer territory (https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/taint-and-toleration/)
- Crash back-off can be tuned (alpha options: start at 1 s, cap at 60 s): Ch 12 (https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/workloads/pods/pod-lifecycle/)
- Managed node pools can auto-repair broken machines (replace the VM itself), a provider loop below Kubernetes' own: Ch 14 (model; not fetched)
- Liveness probes restart a container that is running but hung; readiness keeps traffic off a pod until it's ready: Ch 6 (https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/overview/ "Self-healing" bullet)
- The scheduler's factors (resource requirements, affinity, data locality, deadlines): Ch 5 (https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/architecture/)
- The EndpointSlice controller is the loop that keeps a Service's list of pod addresses current: Ch 7's loop lens (https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/architecture/)
- Pods may hold a helper ("sidecar") container next to the app, which restarts on its own rules: Ch 12/13 (https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/workloads/pods/)
- Engineers recommend Deployments over bare ReplicaSets; a Deployment owns ReplicaSets and swaps them during a rollout: Ch 6 (https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/workloads/controllers/replicaset/)
- Admission checks validate and can reject or modify a request before it's recorded: Ch 11's "gate in front of the loops" (https://kubernetes.io/docs/reference/command-line-tools-reference/kube-apiserver/)
- Spec vs. status can have separate permissions (users write the spec, controllers write the status): Ch 11 RBAC (https://github.com/kubernetes/community/blob/master/contributors/devel/sig-architecture/api-conventions.md)
- Declarative configuration in Git plus a syncing loop is GitOps: Ch 13 (model; object-management page notes config "can be stored in a source control system such as Git")
- GKE may store the cluster's record in Spanner rather than etcd; backing up the record is a self-hosting concern that managed services absorb: Ch 14 (https://cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/cluster-architecture)
- EKS charges per cluster (pricing tied to version support) and AKS has pricing tiers for cluster management: Ch 14 cost (https://docs.aws.amazon.com/eks/latest/userguide/what-is-eks.html; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/aks/core-aks-concepts)
- The Borg/Kubernetes authors' phrase "control through choreography" vs. "a centralized orchestration system" (verified in Ch 3, not re-read here): optional quote for m-03 (https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/44843.pdf)
- "Cybernetics" shares the Greek root of Kubernetes (steersman); a nice aside if sourced: Ch 4 aside at most (model)
